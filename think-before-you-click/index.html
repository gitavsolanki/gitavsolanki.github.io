What Is Phishing?

Phishing is when someone pretends to be a trustworthy person or organisation to trick you into sharing information, clicking a harmful link or opening a dangerous file.

Look beyond the sender's name. A message can appear official while asking you to do something unsafe. Explore the four warning signs below.

Explore the warning signs

0 of 4 warning signs explored

Attackers may use familiar names or addresses that resemble a genuine organisation.

Workplace example: An email claims to be from IT, but its domain does not match your organisation's usual address.

Phishing messages may create fear or urgency so that you act before checking.

Workplace example: “Your account will be locked in 20 minutes unless you respond.”

Unexpected links can take you to imitation websites designed to collect passwords or other details.

Workplace example: An unexpected message asks you to sign in through a link rather than your normal trusted route.

Unexpected attachments may contain harmful content. Check with the sender using a trusted method before opening them.

Workplace example: A document claiming to be from HR asks you to enable macros.

Macro safety: Some documents contain macros — automated actions that can run inside a file. Criminals may use malicious macros to run harmful code. If an unexpected email asks you to enable macros, stop and verify the request through a trusted workplace channel.

Stop. Check. Think before you click.

How to Respond and Report

Every organisation has its own reporting process. Explore all three steps to learn what to do when you receive a suspicious message.

0 of 3 steps explored

Do not click suspicious links, open unexpected attachments, reply to the message or share passwords, codes or payment details.

Use your employer's approved phishing-report button, dedicated reporting email address or IT/security team. Follow your workplace procedure.

Follow instructions from IT/security. If you already clicked, shared credentials or sent money, report it immediately so they can help contain the risk.

Stop. Verify independently. Report using the approved channel.
